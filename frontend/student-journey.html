Newton’s Second Law

The acceleration produced by a net force is proportional to the force and inversely proportional to mass.

F = m × a
Example course excerpt (illustrative)
Guided tutoring / Example lesson

Ask. Think. Understand.

Pick a question and see how a tutor would use the same course reference.

A heavier object has more inertia. With the same net force, a larger mass produces a smaller acceleration (a = F / m).

If mass stays constant and you double the net force, acceleration doubles.

First identify the forces. Choose coordinate axes, write the force balance for each direction, and solve step by step.

Newton's Second Law

Motion changes when an external net force acts on a body. The acceleration of the body is directly proportional to the net force, and inversely proportional to the mass of the body, where the mass is assumed constant...

∑ F = ma
